## Lessons from Improv

The further I get into improv, the more life lessons I find.

1. As Mick Napier explains, the rules of improv aren't actually rules you follow to create a good scene. They're pieces of "advice" collected by people who have been in and seen bad scenes as "not-to-do's". Doing those things will not necessarily lead to a good scene. The only way to really be in a good scene, among many things, is being in the scene, in the present, listening well, building up the energy, and yes-anding. In life, I've given advice to underclassmen and received advice from people older than me. As you would with the rules of improve, take the advice with grains of salt. Know that they are less guiderails to living a good life and more reflections on experiences other people have scene.

2. Overthinking about the scene will pull you out of the scene and make you unfunny. For instance, thinking about how a scene is becoming a "talking heads" scene will pull you out of a scene and make it worse. Focus on being in the present, listening well, and building onto the scene. Stay consistent with your character. Make unexpected decisions. The funny will come. The same happens in life.

3. You have to put in the work upfront to make a show enjoyable for the audience and fun for your troupe members. If you don't, your troupe members will be worrying about the state of the show during the show, and the audience will feel it to. This means preparing lighting, call scripts, and warm ups for the audience. The details matter. Lay the groundwork of a show well so that your troupe members can focus on playing and your audience on watching (not thinking about how the show is awkward).

4. Improv is and isn't that deep. I and the people around me do improv because it's fun. It adds to our lives outside of classes. It's a fun, chill, lit time. But the moment it becomes a chore, it loses its spark. That's not to say it'll feel fun all the time, or that I won't experience off days, but we need to maintain the balance of learning more and improving with not taking ourselves too seriously.

5. Have faith in people. They will also experience on and off days. Believe in them, help them the best you can by giving offers, and be patient.

6. It's ok to leave old ideas behind. If the scene is moving somewhere, move with it and adapt. Trying to hold onto a plot point that has been left purposefully in the past will only make the scene awkward. If the callback feels right in the story, go for it. But feel free to grow out of old project ideas and industry interests. If new interests call, it is more than ok to follow them. The world and scene are changing.

7. Don't try to be funny or high energy. Trying to be funny every offer or high energy every scene will drain your audience. Sometimes the drama and tension makes the comedic break more enjoyable.

I also took some acting classes in hopes it'd help with my improv. I've found it surprisingly fun regardless. Here are some takeaways from acting:

1. When you feel yourself diving into the character and emotion, and you're surprising yourself, lean in further. Don't run away from it. Lean into the scary and unexpected.

2. Jump into the monologue even if you don't fully feel ready. Even if you feel yourself just on the cusp of that emotion. And if you need to yell or shout or jump to get yourself into that emotion before you start, go do that.

3. Relax:)
